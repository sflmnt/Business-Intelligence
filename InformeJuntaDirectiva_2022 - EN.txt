The Colombian economy in 2022 was bittersweet. This is because, despite the improvement in growth that occurred during this period, the expected results were not achieved. That year, its growth was less than desired. It was expected not to exceed the limits, but it was too little to achieve the benefits that Colombia would expect upon achieving these results. The GDP (Gross Domestic Product) was estimated to have grown during the year, but many economic sectors also experienced declines.

Entities such as DANE (National Administrative Department of Statistics), MHCP (Ministry of Finance and Public Credit), BRC (Bank of the Republic of Colombia), the government, and others conducted a very detailed quarterly analysis of the country's various sectors, including the economy.

The statistics obtained for the country's economic sector for 2022 are not entirely negative, but neither are they entirely positive. EL PAIS América Colombia published the following in an informative article:

Colombia's National Administrative Department of Statistics (DANE) revealed the official growth figure for the country's Gross Domestic Product: 7.5%. The figure, very positive in typical economic contexts, is bittersweet. Bitter because the Ministry of Finance estimated growth of 8.2% and the Bank of the Republic estimated 8.1%, so the increase was lower than expected. This is a sweet spot because one of the government's and experts' major concerns was excessive growth in 2022 that would lead to stagnation in 2023, and a more moderate pace last year could mean a less severe slowdown this year.

In another context, it can be seen that Colombia was also economically affected by a global economic phenomenon, inflation, that year; since 2021, there had been a shift in the consumer price index (CPI), due to the supply impacts left by the pandemic. What the Colombian markets envisioned was that the year would close with inflation of approximately 12.5%, and that next year it would remain at a high level, close to 7%.

Although the expected results were not achieved that year, it was confirmed that a very significant level of economic growth was achieved throughout the Latin American region. This was due to the figure published by DANE, which concluded that the economy grew 7.5% at the end of 2022. This means that more than $1.4 trillion pesos in goods and services were produced.

Undoubtedly, the factors that enabled this significant growth were:

Wholesale and retail trade.

Air and ground transportation.

Accommodation and food services.
All of these strongly influenced the Colombian economy, but in this context, the category that made the greatest contribution was the manufacturing industry, especially the textile sector, which was one of the largest drivers of employment in Colombia. Other influential factors in the fourth quarter of the year were:

Entertainment.

Concerts.

The World Cup.

Gaming and gambling.

It is worth noting that the second quarter of 2022 was the most dynamic for the economy, especially between April and May, continuing until August. By September, the economy began to show signs of slowing.

According to DANE (National Statistics Institute), agriculture, which has maintained a negative trend in recent years, and mining were the most affected sectors during 2022. Household spending was one of the main factors driving and driving the economy, ahead of public spending. All of this represents progress in Colombia's economic growth, as in years prior to 2022, growth was approximately 3.2%.

Given the dynamic performance of some sectors in industry, commerce, and the arts, Colombia ranked first in terms of growth among all UN countries.
