International Macroeconomic Environment

The global economy expanded at an annual rate of nearly 2.6% in the first quarter of 2023 due to improved consumer confidence, the normalization of global supply chains, and favorable dynamics in services activities.
A slowdown in global growth is anticipated for the remainder of the year amid an uncertain economic climate characterized by geopolitical tensions, risks to financial stability, high debt in many economies, and the effects of restrictive monetary policies in numerous countries, whose inflation rates remain above their targets, despite the declines achieved so far.
The IMF forecasts that the moderating inflation trend will continue in the coming months, with average annual global inflation falling from 8.7% in 2022 to 6.8% in 2023.

Economic Activity in Colombia

After achieving strong growth of 7.3% in 2022, GDP growth slowed in the first quarter of 2023, reaching an annualized figure of 3.0%, driven by positive net external demand, primarily due to a drop in imports, especially capital goods, affected by the slowdown in economic activity.
Domestic demand contributed negatively to growth, reflected in the loss of momentum in household consumption and the contraction of gross capital formation.
The technical team at the Central Bank forecasts GDP growth of 0.9% in 2023.

Employment

The unemployment rate continued to decline year-on-year during the first months of 2023, reaching 10.4% in the moving quarter ending in May, below its historical average, largely due to the recovery of employment in rural areas.
The informality rate stood at 57.3% in May 2023, 0.3 percentage points higher than the 57% observed in December 2022.
By economic sector, the largest contributions to the annual change in employment, in the national aggregate, were made by the trade and lodging, manufacturing, and professional activities.

Inflation and Monetary Policy

Consumer price inflation maintained an upward trend throughout the previous year, reaching 13.1% at the end of 2022. During the first quarter of 2023, inflation stabilized at around 13.3% annually and began to show a downward trend starting in April, reaching 12.1% annually in June, one percentage point below the level recorded in December 2022.
The declines in the inflation rate during the second quarter were explained, among other factors, by the drop in food inflation; downward adjustments in international prices for some foods, raw materials, and transportation costs; the appreciation of the exchange rate; and the reduction in producer price inflation, offset by the rise in fuel prices.

Balance of Payments

In the first quarter of 2023, the current account deficit was 4.2% of GDP. This meant a reduction in the external imbalance of two percentage points of GDP, compared to that recorded in the same quarter of 2022.
This decrease was primarily due to the reduction in the goods trade deficit and the lower imbalance in services trade.
For 2023, the technical team projects a current account deficit of 4.1% of GDP, lower than the 6.2% deficit observed in 2022.

International Reserves

So far in 2023, there has been an increase in the level of net international reserves, which totaled USD 57,705.7 million (m) as of May 31, 2023, representing an increase of USD 436.6 million compared to the amount recorded at the end of 2022. This is due to the positive profitability obtained during this period, resulting from the higher returns received. The appreciation of investments has also contributed to the increase in the level of reserves. These positive impacts on investments have been partially offset by the devaluation of other reserve currencies against the US dollar, which has generated a negative exchange rate effect on the valuation of reserves.

Bank of the Republic's Earnings

In the first half of 2023, the Bank of the Republic's earnings amounted to COP 4,049 billion (mm), as a result of income of COP 6,423 billion and expenses of COP 2,374 billion.
Income was mainly due to the return on international reserves and, to a lesser extent, to the return on the TES held by the Bank and on expansionary repos. Expenditures are mainly explained by the remuneration of government deposits with the Bank and contractionary deposits, and by current expenses.

Corporate.
A profit of COP 8,408 billion is projected for 2023, higher than that obtained in 2022 (COP 1,506 billion), which would come primarily from the return on international reserves. The expected return on reserves would respond to high external interest rates.

Public Finance

The fiscal deficit of the Central National Government (CNG) stood at 5.3% of GDP in 2022, which implied a correction of 1.7 percentage points compared to the 2021 deficit (7.0% of GDP). With this result, the CNG achieved the deficit target established for the transition period in the Fiscal Rule (8.3%).
This adjustment was possible thanks to the dynamism of the Colombian economy, which boosted tax collection, favorable terms of trade, and the reduction of spending pressures associated with the COVID-19 pandemic.
