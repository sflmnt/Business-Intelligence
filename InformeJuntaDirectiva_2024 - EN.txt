During the first quarter of 2024, economic activity in Colombia maintained the recovery trend it exhibited in the last quarter of 2023. After the 0.7% contraction in gross domestic product (GDP) in the third quarter of 2023, this variable grew by 0.4% in the fourth quarter of last year and 0.9% between January and March of this year. Although this growth rate remains moderate, the forecasts of the technical team and various analysts indicate that it will consolidate in the coming months, approaching its potential capacity in 2025. Furthermore, the annual inflation rate made progress in its convergence to the target during the first half of the year, declining from 9.3% in December to 7.4% in March, and stabilizing around 7.2% in the second quarter. Likewise, the country's external imbalance continued to narrow, with a current account deficit of 1.9% of GDP recorded in the first three months of the year, which was lower than that of 2023 (2.5% of GDP) and that observed in the same period of the previous year (3.7% of GDP). Regarding public finances, the significant correction of the fiscal imbalance achieved the previous year stands out, which resulted in a reduction in the General Government deficit from 6.3% to 2.7% of GDP between 2022 and 2023. Since December 2023, these macroeconomic advances have been accompanied by the start of a new monetary policy cycle, with progressive interest rate reductions. The decision to undertake this policy change stems from the consensus reached among the co-directors of the Board of Directors of the Banco de la República (JDBR) that favorable conditions have been created for adopting a less contractionary policy stance, as confirmed by the downward trend shown by both annual inflation and inflation expectations since April 2023, as well as the correction of the external deficit toward a more sustainable balance.
While these favorable conditions are identified, some significant risks to inflation's convergence to its 3% target are noted. These include the downward rigidity of inflation in some services, including rents and energy rates, as well as the risks of a depreciation of the peso against the dollar due to changing conditions in the external context, the persisting uncertainty regarding monetary policy in the United States, and local public policy issues. Likewise, there is the risk related to food prices, whose inflation spiked in May and June due to the effects of the El Niño phenomenon on the harvests of certain perishable foods. Additionally, while inflation expectations show a downward trend over time, they remain above the target for the relevant timeframes in which the Board of Directors (BDBR) aims to achieve it. Furthermore, the expected increase in the Central National Government (CNG) deficit in 2024 could make it difficult for inflation to converge toward its target within the timeframe in which the Board of Directors (BDBR) hopes to achieve it. The existence of the aforementioned risks to inflation behavior requires the monetary authority to be especially cautious with the amount of policy interest rate cuts. This is because there is a risk that very rapid declines may have to be reversed, at significant credibility costs, if events materialize that make it difficult to achieve the inflation target within the timeframes set by the Board of Directors. Hence the importance of each decision using the most recent available information as the basis for a detailed assessment of inflation behavior and expectations; observed and expected economic growth; and other key variables that determine the country's macroeconomic stability and the convergence of inflation toward its target. This task is regularly carried out by the technical team of the Banco de la República for each Board of Directors session.
